# Why Brooklyn brownstone neighborhoods?

> A trust-based marketplace only works if **density** is real. Density of
> *homeowners*, specifically, not just population. That's why we're not
> launching in Manhattan, not launching in Chicago, and not launching
> nationwide.

This doc justifies the beachhead. It'll get revisited every time someone (us,
an investor, a competitor) argues we should expand faster.

## The criteria for a great beachhead

We need a place that scores high on all five of these:

1. **Homeowner density per square mile.** The neighbor-trust feature is worthless
   if your nearest reviewer is two miles away.
2. **Neighbor identity culture.** People need to think of themselves as part of a
   block, not part of an anonymous building. Without that, the "review from your
   neighbor" framing loses its emotional pull.
3. **Job frequency.** Old houses break more than new condos. Old homeowners
   maintain more than first-time buyers. We want both.
4. **Walkable supply acquisition.** In the first 6 months we will be
   hand-recruiting pros. We need to physically reach them at hardware stores,
   depots, and coffee shops.
5. **Founder proximity.** Two of three founders are at Columbia. Whatever we pick
   has to be reachable by subway in under 90 minutes, ideally under 45.

## How Brooklyn brownstone neighborhoods score

| Criterion | Brownstone Brooklyn | Manhattan | Queens | NJ suburbs | Chicago |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Homeowner density | High | Low | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| Neighbor culture | **Very high** | Low | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| Job frequency | High | Medium | High | High | High |
| Walkable supply | Yes | Partial | Partial | No | Partial |
| Founder proximity | Yes (25 min) | Yes (10 min) | Yes (30 min) | Partial | No |

Brownstone Brooklyn is the only row with no weak link.

## Specific target neighborhoods (Brooklyn Community Districts 6, 8, 2, 3)

In rough priority order for the first wedge:

1. **Park Slope** (CD 6). High homeownership in row houses, strong
   block-association culture, family-heavy, lots of pre-war maintenance jobs.
2. **Cobble Hill, Carroll Gardens, Boerum Hill** (CD 6). Extremely dense
   brownstone fabric, walkable shops, established trust networks.
3. **Brooklyn Heights** (CD 2). Older homeowners, big maintenance budgets,
   classic pre-war housing stock.
4. **Fort Greene and Clinton Hill** (CD 2). Mix of long-term homeowners and
   recent renovators, strong neighborhood identity.
5. **Crown Heights and Prospect Heights** (CD 8, 6). Mid-renovation phase, many
   first-time homeowners who don't have a trusted plumber yet.
6. **Bed-Stuy** (CD 3). Large brownstone stock, fast-renovating, latent demand.

## Why not Manhattan

Manhattan has the population but not the homeowner density. Most renters of
luxury buildings call the super. Most condo owners use the building's
preferred-vendor list. The unit economics get worse, not better, with population
density when most of the population doesn't decide who fixes the sink.

That said: we'll grab Manhattan brownstone-equivalent neighborhoods (West
Village, UWS pre-war, Harlem brownstones) once supply density in Brooklyn is
real. The neighbor-trust idea transfers fine. It doesn't need to be a
brownstone, it needs to be a block where neighbors talk.

## Why not the suburbs

Suburban jobs are larger ($800 fence repair, $5,000 deck rebuild) but supply
acquisition costs go up dramatically. Pros drive between jobs and don't cluster
at coffee shops. Founder visit-cost is much higher. We'll do suburbs in v1.x.
Great expansion target, not a great beachhead.

## Why not Chicago, Philly, or DC

The brownstone-equivalent neighborhoods (Lincoln Park, Society Hill, Capitol
Hill) look great on paper. But none of us live there. The "do things that don't
scale" phase requires being on the ground every day, and we can't be.

## What we lose by starting narrow

- A bigger TAM headline number for fundraising decks. (We'll write the
  Brownstone-Brooklyn to Brownstone-NYC to US-walkable-homeowner-neighborhoods to
  US-broad expansion story explicitly.)
- The temptation to chase TAM before nailing trust.
- A few competitive months. Thumbtack and Angi have access to all these
  neighborhoods already. **But none of them is *focused* on this segment**,
  which is exactly why we can win it.

## What we win by starting narrow

- Real reviews from real neighbors within months, not years.
- A defensible local network effect: the next pro to enter Park Slope has to
  beat a roster of pros that already have 50+ reviews from people on the same
  block.
- A founding story you can tell honestly: "We made it work on one block. Then
  five. Then a neighborhood. Then a borough."
- A press hook: "Brooklyn brownstone startup."

## Decision rule for expansion

We don't open a new neighborhood until the current one is *liquid*, which we're
defining as:

- 25 or more verified pros across the 5 trades
- 50 or more active homeowner accounts
- 1+ successful job per active homeowner-month on average
- 3.0+ reviews per booked job (capture rate)

When a neighborhood hits liquidity, we open the next adjacent one. We expand
the way a brownstone neighborhood actually expands: one block at a time, by
referral.
